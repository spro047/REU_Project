Glossary — What Everything Means

Before diving into the numbers, here's a plain-language guide to all the technical terms. No medical or engineering background needed.

What is Suturing?

Suturing (also called stitching) is the medical technique of closing a wound or cut in the skin using thread and a needle. Think of it like sewing, but on a patient's skin. Doctors and medical students practice this skill on artificial skin pads (pink rubber-like pads with grid lines) before doing it on real patients. This dataset contains photos of those practice sessions.

THE 6 QUALITY METRICS (Rated 1-10)

Overall
"How good does this entire suture line look overall?" — A global grade combining everything into one score.
Like grading an entire painting — not perfect, but pretty good.
ISD (Inter-Suture Distance)
"Are the stitches evenly spaced apart?" — Measures consistency of gaps between each stitch.
Like soldiers standing in a line — some are perfectly spaced, others are too close or too far apart.
Slack
"Is the thread tight enough, or is it loose and floppy?" — Measures thread tension control.
Like a guitar string — too loose sounds bad, too tight breaks. Just right is perfect.
Position
"Did the needle go in and come out at the right spot?" — Measures placement accuracy.
Like parking a car — you want it centered in the spot, not crooked or too far to one side.
Angulation
"Is the needle going in at the right angle?" — Measures the angle of needle entry and exit.
Like throwing a ball — the angle matters. Too steep or too flat won't work well.
Width
"Are all the stitches the same width/size?" — Measures consistency of stitch dimensions.
Like handwriting — consistent letters look professional, messy sizes look sloppy.

OTHER TERMS YOU'LL SEE

Cohort
A group of images grouped together for a specific purpose — like "Class A" vs "Class B".
Training Set
The practice exam — images used to teach the AI what good vs bad sutures look like.
Validation Set
The quiz — images used to check if the AI learned correctly.
Annotation
The expert's grade/score for each image — like a teacher marking an exam.
Correlation
How closely two things are related. High correlation = they usually go together. Low = they're independent.
Outlier
An unusual score that's very different from most others — like a student who scores 2/10 when everyone else gets 6-8.
Standard Deviation (SD)
How spread out the scores are. Small SD = everyone scored similarly. Large SD = big range of scores.
Skewness
Whether scores lean toward high or low. "Left-skewed" means most scores are on the higher end.

Why This Research Matters

This research helps create an AI assistant that can automatically evaluate suture quality, giving medical students instant feedback on their practice. Instead of waiting for a teacher to check their work, students can get immediate, consistent grading — like having a personal coach available 24/7. The AI learns from thousands of examples scored by experts, then can grade new sutures on its own.

Critical Insights for AI Framework

Model Design

Multi-task learning across all 6 metrics. Prioritize Slack as primary feature due to highest correlation with Overall (r=0.722).

Feature Engineering

Consider composite score: Slack + Width + Position as "Core Quality Index". ISD appears independent — handle separately.

Data Challenges

Address class imbalance for Doctor 7. Consider data augmentation or weighted sampling. Validate cross-cohort generalization.
